# Mantara Commercial Pricing and Service Model

**Status:** Commercial planning draft

**Last updated:** 9 August 2026

**Decision owner:** Mantara leadership

**Review point:** After the first three paid pilots

## Executive summary

Mantara should enter the market with a paid, guided pilot rather than a permanent free plan. The recommended pricing unit is the mining organization and site, with an allowance for people who can sign in. Worker records, equipment records, and production entries should not be charged as seats. This keeps the price connected to operational value and avoids penalizing a customer for recording its full workforce.

The first commercial offer should be:

- a 90-day pilot at **TZS 3,000,000** for one site;
- **Starter** at **TZS 750,000 per month** for one small mine;
- **Growth** at **TZS 2,500,000 per month** for up to three sites; and
- **Enterprise** from **TZS 7,500,000 per month**, with the exact scope agreed in a statement of work.

These are pricing hypotheses, not a final public rate card. They should be tested with 3–5 Tanzania-based mining design partners and revised using measured customer value, delivery cost, product usage, and willingness to pay.

## 1. Commercial positioning

Mantara is not only maintenance software, a safety checklist, or an accounting tool. It is a bilingual mining operations platform that connects production and ore movement, people, equipment, fuel, inventory, expenses, maintenance, safety, compliance, documents, operational intelligence, and geological evidence.

The initial promise should be simple:

> Mantara gives a mine owner and operating team one trusted daily view of what was produced, used, spent, moved, maintained, and reported.

The strongest entry point is likely production and ore tracking combined with fuel control and daily reporting. Broader modules should support expansion after Mantara proves this first operational value.

## 2. Recommended packages

All prices below exclude applicable taxes, statutory charges, travel, accommodation, third-party services, hardware, and separately scoped custom work.

| Package | Monthly price | Annual prepayment | Included scale | Best for |
| --- | ---: | ---: | --- | --- |
| Paid pilot | TZS 3,000,000 once | Not applicable | 1 site, up to 15 login users, 90 days | A mine proving operational value before committing |
| Starter | TZS 750,000 | TZS 7,500,000 | 1 site, up to 10 login users | A small mine replacing paper, Excel, and WhatsApp reporting |
| Growth | TZS 2,500,000 | TZS 25,000,000 | Up to 3 sites, up to 30 login users | A growing operator needing controls, intelligence, and multi-site visibility |
| Enterprise | From TZS 7,500,000 | From TZS 75,000,000 | Proposed baseline: up to 5 sites and 100 login users | A medium or large operator needing tailored governance and service levels |

Annual prepayment charges for ten months and provides twelve months of service, equivalent to a 16.7% discount. Monthly subscriptions are prepaid.

For planning only, using a rounded exchange rate of **TZS 2,640 per USD**, the monthly prices are approximately USD 284, USD 947, and USD 2,841. Contracts should define their own currency and exchange-rate rule rather than rely on this planning conversion. The rate is rounded from the Bank of Tanzania's published July 2026 exchange rates.

### Paid pilot — TZS 3,000,000 for 90 days

The pilot includes:

- one mine site and up to 15 named users with login access;
- configuration of the organization, site, roles, and initial operating registers;
- one standard data-import workbook;
- two remote training sessions;
- a baseline review, weekly operating check-in, and pilot closeout report;
- core production, ore, workforce, equipment, fuel, inventory, and expense workflows; and
- agreed success measures before the pilot begins.

If the customer signs an annual Starter, Growth, or Enterprise agreement within 30 days of pilot completion, credit **50% of the pilot fee** against the first annual invoice. The pilot should not be free: payment tests whether the problem matters and funds implementation support.

### Starter — TZS 750,000 per month

Starter includes:

- one site and up to 10 named users with login access;
- unlimited worker, equipment, inventory-item, and production records within reasonable-use limits;
- workforce and attendance;
- equipment register;
- production, ore, tonnes, bags, grade, and destination records;
- fuel, inventory, and expense tracking;
- standard dashboards and reports;
- English and Kiswahili; and
- standard support with a two-business-day initial response target.

Starter is intentionally focused. Maintenance planning, safety, compliance, advanced approval controls, operational intelligence, geology, and multi-site management belong in Growth.

### Growth — TZS 2,500,000 per month

Growth includes everything in Starter, plus:

- up to three sites and 30 named users with login access;
- maintenance requests, schedules, and history;
- safety observations and inspections;
- compliance registers and reminders;
- approval workflows, audit history, and private documents;
- cost-per-tonne, cost-per-gram, budget-versus-actual, utilization, and productivity views;
- recovery and revenue assumptions, cash-flow forecast, daily summaries, and grounded Mantara Brain answers;
- offline drafts for supported field workflows;
- samples, assays, drill intervals, licence boundaries, geological files, and the current evidence-bounded GeoAI observations;
- priority support with a one-business-day initial response target; and
- one remote monthly operating review.

### Enterprise — from TZS 7,500,000 per month

Enterprise includes all currently released Growth capabilities, plus:

- a contract-defined multi-site and user allowance;
- tailored roles, governance, approval rules, and report packs using supported product capabilities;
- a named customer-success contact;
- quarterly operating and roadmap reviews;
- a contract-defined support and service-level schedule; and
- separately scoped onboarding, migration, integrations, or custom development.

Enterprise must not imply that unfinished capabilities are available. Single sign-on, custom integrations, computer vision, drones, IoT telemetry, richer spatial analysis, and advanced predictive GeoAI should be sold only after technical discovery and a signed statement of work confirms that they can be delivered.

## 4. How Mantara should deliver the service

### Discover — 1 to 2 weeks

- map the customer's current production, ore, fuel, inventory, people, maintenance, safety, compliance, and reporting workflows;
- agree the sites, users, roles, data sources, and operating language;
- establish baseline measures and pilot success criteria; and
- identify data, connectivity, device, security, and support constraints.

### Implement — normally 2 to 6 weeks

- configure the organization and sites;
- create roles and authorized users;
- prepare and import agreed clean data;
- train managers and field users; and
- complete a go-live checklist and access-control test.

### Prove — 90-day paid pilot

- review usage and data quality weekly;
- compare progress against the baseline;
- resolve adoption issues within the agreed support scope; and
- issue a closeout report showing results, gaps, and the recommended subscription.

### Operate and expand

- move successful pilots to annual subscriptions;
- hold monthly reviews for Growth and quarterly reviews for Enterprise;
- add sites or services only when usage and operational ownership are healthy; and
- agree all new integrations or custom work separately.

## 5. Pilot success measures

Each pilot should select a small set of measurable outcomes, such as:

- percentage of operating days with a complete daily production record;
- time from shift close to management visibility;
- reconciliation rate for tonnes, bags, grade, and destination;
- reduction in missing fuel or inventory records;
- percentage of active equipment with current status and maintenance history;
- time spent preparing the daily or weekly management report;
- weekly active login users and completion of role-specific workflows; and
- zero confirmed cross-tenant data-access incidents.

The customer and Mantara should agree baseline values, targets, evidence sources, and responsible owners before go-live.

## 6. Pricing logic and guardrails

### Charge for value, not database volume

- Price the operating relationship by organization and site.
- Count named people who can log in, not all workers stored in the system.
- Keep ordinary operational records within reasonable-use limits.
- Scope unusually high storage, mapping, integration, or support demand separately.

### Protect the economics

Until actual cost data is available, use these internal targets:

- at least 75% gross margin on recurring subscriptions;
- at least 50% gross margin on professional services;
- customer-acquisition payback within six months;
- Starter onboarding of no more than 12 team hours and support below two hours per month;
- Growth onboarding of no more than 30 team hours and support below five hours per month; and
- dedicated Enterprise delivery priced separately rather than absorbed into the subscription.

These are operating targets, not customer-facing guarantees.

### Use disciplined discounts

- A design partner may receive up to 30% off for no more than 12 months in return for scheduled product feedback and, if successful, permission to develop a reference or case study.
- Discounts expire automatically and must not become permanent list prices.
- Prefer reducing scope or phasing the rollout over discounting a package that the customer cannot justify.
- Founder, reseller, cooperative, nonprofit, or strategic pricing requires a written business reason and an expiry or review date.

### Connect price to measurable customer value

As a working rule, recurring fees should represent roughly **10–20% of the measurable monthly value** Mantara helps create or protect. Examples include reduced fuel or inventory variance, fewer reporting hours, earlier detection of equipment problems, quicker reconciliation of ore movements, and avoided compliance administration. This rule must be tested with actual pilot data; it is not a claim that Mantara already produces those savings.

## 7. Market reference points

Comparable products are only directional because they solve narrower problems and use different packaging:

- MaintainX lists Essential at USD 20 per user per month and Premium at USD 65 when billed annually.
- UpKeep lists Essential at USD 24 per user per month and Premium at USD 55, with higher plans quoted.
- SafetyCulture lists its Full seat at USD 24 per month when billed annually.

At ten paid users, these point tools imply roughly USD 200–650 per month before implementation and add-ons. Mantara Starter at about USD 284 per month sits within that range while using a site-led model and covering a wider mining workflow. This comparison supports testing the proposed price; it does not prove willingness to pay in Tanzania.

## 9. Commercial and contract rules

Before accepting a paying customer, the order form or contract should define:

- legal entities, sites, package, term, price, currency, taxes, and payment schedule;
- included users, implementation, training, support, and service levels;
- customer data ownership, authorized access, retention, export, and deletion;
- security responsibilities, backup and recovery expectations, and incident escalation;
- third-party hosting, messaging, maps, storage, or integration costs;
- product availability, planned versus delivered capabilities, and acceptable use;
- change requests, custom intellectual property, and maintenance of custom work;
- renewal, price changes, suspension, termination, and exit assistance; and
- clear statements that Mantara is not legal, financial, geological-certification, or safety-professional advice.

Subscriptions should be prepaid. A standard implementation payment schedule is **60% at signature and 40% before go-live**. Exact contract language and tax treatment require review by qualified Tanzanian legal and tax professionals before commercial launch.

## 10. Evidence required before publishing prices

Mantara should complete the following before turning this draft into a public rate card:

1. Run 3–5 paid pilots across small mining, processing, or quarry operations.
2. Record onboarding hours, support hours, infrastructure cost, data-migration effort, and product usage by customer.
3. Measure baseline and closing operational outcomes.
4. Interview the buyer separately from daily users about value, budget, approval process, and renewal conditions.
5. Test at least two package or price presentations without promising discounts in advance.
6. Confirm legal terms, tax treatment, invoice process, support ownership, backup/recovery, and pilot readiness.
7. Convert at least three pilots and document why each customer bought, delayed, rejected, or churned.
8. Approve Pricing v1, sales materials, and discount authority, then review them quarterly during the first commercial year.

## 11. Open decisions

- Whether Starter customers need a limited maintenance view or should upgrade to Growth for all maintenance workflows.
- Whether additional sites should always require Growth, instead of being sold as a Starter add-on.
- Whether WhatsApp support can be operationally sustained and audited as customer volume grows.
- Which support hours and response targets are realistic before signing an Enterprise service-level agreement.
- Whether geological storage needs a separate storage allowance and overage price.
- Which partner or reseller commission structure works without weakening customer success or margins.

## Caveats

- The prices are commercial hypotheses based on Mantara's present product scope and directional software benchmarks; they are not validated customer willingness-to-pay data.
- Competitor plans are not directly equivalent to Mantara and may change.
- Currency equivalents are planning illustrations, not contract quotations.
- Product capabilities must be reconfirmed at sale time. Roadmap items must not be represented as delivered features.
- Professional legal, tax, security, mining, safety, and geological review remains necessary where applicable.
